# About the founder and the project's history

🇬🇧 English · [🇵🇱 Polski](ABOUT.pl.md)

## Tomasz Maciąg — founder of HELIMAC

I am a hands-on practitioner rather than a trained scientist. For many years I worked in refrigeration and building services. At the Bydgoszcz refrigeration equipment factory BYFUCH and at Impuls, I designed and built refrigeration units, including for promotional projects for brands such as Lech, Warka, Żywiec and Pepsi-Cola. I also ran my own business in refrigeration, air conditioning, ventilation, heat recovery and automation.

Alongside my job I have always developed my own inventions. My passion is combining technologies from different fields — even ones that seem to have nothing in common.

## The first HeliMac (2013–2015)

The name HELIMAC combines **HELI**copter and **MAC**iąg. It is not new: in 2013–2015 I worked on **HeliMac**, a concept for a single-person electric vertical take-off and landing vehicle.

- I built a team and ran powertrain tests.
- I worked with, among others, **Military Aviation Works No. 2 (WZL-2) in Bydgoszcz**, whose designers developed the aluminium skeleton of the HeliMac model; the **KOMEL Institute of Electrical Drives and Machines** in Katowice; and the **Composite Structures Laboratory of the Silesian Science and Technology Centre of the Aviation Industry**.
- 2014 — presented on Polish Radio PiK; qualified for the next round of the Polish TV inventors' show "Talent, zgłoś się!" (production was cancelled in early 2015).
- 23 May 2015 — HeliMac featured on the TVP1 popular-science programme "Jak to działa?" ("How does it work?"), episode 88.
- An Indiegogo campaign.

I funded the project mostly from private savings. Without an investor it could not be taken to a finished machine, and I had to set inventing aside and focus on paid work. Today, watching vehicles like Jetson ONE emerge, I can see that the direction I was working on over a decade ago had a future.

## A lesson: timing matters

When launching a startup or bringing a breakthrough technology to market, the idea alone is not enough — **timing** matters just as much. Too early, and the market is not ready to accept it. Too late, and others are already ahead. You have to find the right moment.

I believe HeliMac in 2013–2015 was simply **too early**. The idea was good, and with an investor HeliMac could have flown before vehicles such as [Jetson ONE](https://jetson.com/) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jetson_One)). Jetson was founded in 2017 and its first prototype flew in early 2018 — several years after I started working on HeliMac. Interestingly, Jetson has Polish roots too: co-founder Tomasz Patan built and flew the first prototype in Poland. It shows the direction was right. **HeliMac was already in the Polish media in 2014–2015 — on radio (Polish Radio PiK, 2014), on Polish national television (TVP1, "Jak to działa?", 2015) and online (Komputer Świat, YouTube, Indiegogo)** — several years before Jetson was founded. I don't know whether anyone behind Jetson came across it, but I like to think such ideas were in the air and may have inspired others.

Today the moment is different: the eVTOL market exists, batteries, motors and power electronics have improved enormously, and AI gives a single person capabilities that once required a whole team.

I also worked on other projects, including **D+Sensor** (from 2013), a wrist-worn device concept to warn of hypoglycaemia, developed with Dr. Tomasz Marciniak of the then University of Technology and Life Sciences in Bydgoszcz, Prof. Krzysztof Narkiewicz and specialists from the Medical University of Gdańsk.

## HELIMAC today — why now

Today's HELIMAC **continues that project, but it is a new design**: a single-seat electric coaxial helicopter, designed from scratch and published as open source.

The difference between 2015 and today is **artificial intelligence**. Back then, moving a project forward needed a whole team, and every one of those people cost money. Today a creative person carries tools in their phone that support work once split among many specialists:

| Then — separate people | Today — supported by AI |
|---|---|
| mechanical design engineer | assembly concepts, bearing selection, strength calculations |
| electronics / power-electronics engineer | schematics and PCBs (MC-P inverter), component selection, thermal budget |
| electric-machine designer | motor sizing, trade studies (e.g. axial vs. radial flux) |
| aerodynamicist / rotor specialist | rotor sizing, hover power, flight time |
| battery engineer | 400 V pack, BMS, energy budget |
| software developer | control software, calculators, tests |
| manufacturing engineer | process selection, preparing files for production |
| graphic and 3D designer | visualisations, drawings, illustrations |
| cost estimator, buyer | requests for quotation, offer comparison, delivered cost |
| project manager | schedule, priorities, critical path, decision log |
| regulatory specialist | review of aviation regulations |
| technical writer, translator | documentation in English and Polish |
| PR / communications | presenting the project, community communication |

**What AI does not replace:** physical tests, measurements, making the parts, review by experienced engineers, and regulatory compliance. That is why this project is open: we are looking for people to check the calculations, challenge the solutions and help verify them in practice. See [SAFETY.md](SAFETY.md).

This time I want to go from an idea to a real, flying machine.

— Tomasz Maciąg, Bydgoszcz, Poland
