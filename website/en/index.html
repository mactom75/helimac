A single-seat electric helicopter, steered like a hang glider.

HELIMAC is a coaxial helicopter with an electric powertrain, designed in Bydgoszcz, Poland, to be as simple as possible: easy to transport, to fly and to maintain. The pilot steers by shifting body weight. The project is open — we publish drawings, calculations, software, test results and mistakes.

Inspiration: Franz Schöffmann’s backpack helicopter

HELIMAC draws on one of the simplest helicopters ever flown: the single-person machine built by Dr. Franz Schöffmann of Stockerau, Austria, flying since around 2004.

Two counter-rotating rotors above the pilot’s head, the drive right below them, and a frame of three tubes that serve as both landing gear and handles. No cabin, no tail rotor. The pilot hangs in a harness and steers by shifting body weight — just like a hang glider. Built in a home workshop, it inspired amateur builders around the world.

Sources: flight video on YouTube, “Another attempt, back-pack helicopter” thread on RotaryForum.

What we take from it

Simplicity
As few parts and mechanisms as possible. A part that isn’t there can’t fail.
Weight-shift flying
You change direction by leaning your body, intuitively, as on a hang glider.
Easy transport
A machine you can take apart and carry in an ordinary car.
Safety
Fly low and slow, test in stages. Safety is a design goal in its own right, not an assumption.

The differences: HELIMAC has an electric powertrain instead of a combustion engine — fewer parts, less noise, no exhaust. It also has variable blade pitch set by two electric actuators. On loss of power the pitch drops automatically, which by calculation allows an autorotative landing.

Design rev J2

Two counter-rotating rotors on one axis, no tail rotor. An electric motor drives them through a cycloidal transmission; the MC-P inverter and control electronics are designed in-house.

The controls are simple: the pilot changes direction by shifting body weight, because rigid hubs carry the moment from the rotors to the frame. Blade pitch (collective) is set by a swashplate driven by two electric actuators, and a pitch difference between the upper and lower rotor turns the machine about its vertical axis. There is no tail rotor and no cyclic pitch.

The target is the ultralight category of Annex I to Regulation (EU) 2018/1139: a single-seat helicopter with a maximum take-off mass of up to 300 kg, which falls under national rules rather than EASA certification.

Build plan

The test order follows the safety rules: a person gets on board only after bench tests, tethered tests and uncrewed flights.

  1. Powertrain and power supply

    In progress. Target: 50 hours on the dynamometer.

    • 400 V design review of the MC-P rev D1 inverter
    • MC-C control board design
    • manufacture of the motor, cycloidal transmission and inverter
    • dynamometer: 395 N·m for 30 minutes, then 50 hours

  2. Airframe

    Frame, collective and swashplate, blades; blade modal test.

  3. Tethered rotor tests

    No pilot, inside a defined safety zone.

  4. Uncrewed flights

    The machine in the air, nobody on board.

  5. Crewed trials

    Only once national regulatory requirements are met.

From HeliMac to HELIMAC

2013–2015

The name combines helicopter and Maciąg. Tomasz Maciąg, a hands-on engineer with many years in refrigeration and automation, worked on HeliMac in 2013–2015: a concept for a single-person electric vertical take-off vehicle. He built a team, ran powertrain tests and worked with, among others, Military Aviation Works No. 2 in Bydgoszcz and the KOMEL Institute.

Funded mostly from private savings, the project never found an investor. In hindsight it was simply too early: Jetson was founded in 2017, several years after HeliMac had been covered by the Polish media.

Why now

Today's HELIMAC continues that project, but it is a new design. The difference is artificial intelligence: work that in 2015 had to be split among many paid specialists can now be carried by one person with the help of AI tools.

Then — separate peopleToday — supported by AI

aerodynamicistrotor sizing, hover power, flight time
battery specialist400 V pack, BMS, energy budget

AI does not replace physical tests, measurements, making parts or review by experienced engineers. That is why the project is open. Full story: ABOUT.md.

Safety first

A 4.5 m rotor, a 400 V battery and an uncertified design are three independent sources of lethal risk. Nothing in this project is yet a verified, buildable design. Everything is provided without any warranty.

Rules for testing, high-voltage work and reporting problems: SAFETY.md.
